| Scope & Problem Complexity | Completes well-defined tasks with guidance while building familiarity with the codebase, tooling, and team processes. Shows increasing independence on scoped problems as they grow. | Delivers features independently, participates in design discussions, reviews code, and improves existing components. Owns small-to-medium scoped problems end-to-end. | Leads development of complex features and systems end-to-end, makes architectural tradeoffs, raises engineering quality, mentors others, and drives delivery across multiple engineers and stakeholders. | Solves high-impact, ambiguous problems, leads cross-team technical initiatives, and sets direction for significant areas of the product or platform. Coaches senior engineers and partners closely with product/leadership. | Defines long-term technical vision and guides large-scale system evolution. Sets standards that shape the engineering organization and influences company-wide strategy through technical leadership and decision-making. |
| Execution & Autonomy | Works with guidance; focuses on learning codebase, execution hygiene, task estimation, and steadily increasing independence on assigned deliverables. | Largely self-directed on scoped work; drives feature delivery from kickoff to testing and rollout, sharpens estimation, and takes direct ownership of project outcomes. | Self-directed across multiple people and stakeholders; navigates technical ambiguity, balances velocity with technical debt, and drives operational excellence and project milestones. | Drives cross-team initiatives autonomously; navigates organizational ambiguity, aligns cross-functional stakeholders, and builds leverage via platforms, standards, and enablement. | Operates with total strategic autonomy; sets company-wide technical strategy, eliminates systemic organizational bottlenecks, and creates durable engineering practices at scale. |
| System Design & Craft Depth | Focuses on fundamentals: methodical debugging, testing, clean readable code, and code-review hygiene while working effectively within team processes. | Focuses on maintainability, reliability basics, performance, sound tradeoffs on scoped components, and thoughtful integration of third-party libraries and services. | Deep system-design expertise and architectural judgment; evaluates competing tradeoffs, anticipates failure modes, and leads complex refactors or service migrations. | Designs distributed systems at scale; addresses systemic scalability bottlenecks, defines org-wide architectural patterns, and sets durable technical standards. | Defines foundational technology choices, multi-year architecture, and global reliability and data strategies across multiple product lines and platforms. |
| Technical Leadership & Mentorship | Demonstrates solid fundamentals, strong coachability, asks good clarifying questions, and actively engages in team processes and peer code reviews. | Conducts constructive code reviews, mentors junior engineers, collaborates effectively with cross-functional partners, and contributes to team knowledge sharing. | Mentors and coaches engineers across the team; leads technical design reviews, sets code and operational quality standards, and coordinates delivery across teams. | Coaches senior engineers and partners closely with product leadership; operates across Staff archetypes (Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand) to multiply engineering leverage. | Recognized company-wide technical authority; advises executive leadership, shapes engineering culture and hiring bars, sponsors emerging technical leaders, and mentors Staff+ talent. |
| Interview Signals & Calibration | Signals: Solid fundamentals, coachability, clean code, asks good questions. Story: A bug debugged methodically, or a feature shipped and learned from. Pitfall to avoid: Over-explaining basics; not knowing why a choice was made. | Signals: Independent delivery, sound tradeoffs on scoped work, reliability awareness. Story: A feature owned end-to-end, including testing and rollout. Pitfall to avoid: Describing tasks instead of decisions; no ownership of outcomes. | Signals: System-design depth, architectural tradeoffs, raising team quality, mentoring. Story: A complex system led with tradeoffs weighed and why. Pitfall to avoid: Going broad but shallow; cannot quantify impact or justify alternatives. | Signals: Cross-team influence, ambiguity navigation, leverage, technical strategy. Story: An ambiguous, org-spanning problem scoped and driven to resolution. Pitfall to avoid: Staying in the IC weeds; no evidence of multiplying others' work. | Signals: Org-shaping vision, durable standards, company-wide judgment. Story: A platform/standard defined that changed how many teams work. Pitfall to avoid: Vision with no execution; influence claims without measurable adoption. |
| Representative Project Scope | Implement a small feature (e.g., add a UI button that triggers an API call, including basic frontend changes, a straightforward backend handler, and standard error handling). | Build an authentication flow (login / registration / password reset), integrating a library or service, handling security considerations, instrumenting key metrics, and resolving performance issues. | Lead a refactor from a monolith to multiple services: define boundaries and APIs, plan migrations, preserve contracts, reduce risk, and coordinate rollout across teams. | Drive a data-store migration to address scalability bottlenecks (e.g., PostgreSQL to distributed NoSQL system), coordinate engineering/product/DevOps, manage correctness, and execute phased rollout with rollback plans. | Define and drive adoption of a company-wide framework or platform (e.g., standardizing service templates, observability, or a cloud migration) impacting hundreds of engineers and multiple product lines. |
